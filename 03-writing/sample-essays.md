## Task 1 Samples | 任务1范文

### Sample 1: Line Graph (Band 7) | 范文1：折线图（7分）

**Question**:
"The line graph below shows the amount of electricity generated from renewable and non-renewable sources in a European country between 1980 and 2020."

**Data summary**:
- Coal: 1980 (300 TWh) → 2000 (250 TWh) → 2020 (80 TWh)
- Natural Gas: 1980 (100 TWh) → 2000 (250 TWh) → 2020 (200 TWh)
- Nuclear: 1980 (50 TWh) → 2000 (150 TWh) → 2020 (100 TWh)
- Renewables: 1980 (20 TWh) → 2000 (50 TWh) → 2020 (250 TWh)

**Essay**:

```
The line graph illustrates the amount of electricity generated from four
different sources in a European country over a 40-year period from 1980
to 2020.

Overall, it is evident that while coal was the dominant energy source at
the beginning of the period, it experienced a significant decline by 2020.
In contrast, renewable energy showed the most dramatic growth, becoming
the largest source by the end of the period. Natural gas and nuclear power
both increased initially before following different trajectories.

In 1980, coal was by far the largest source of electricity, generating
approximately 300 terawatt-hours. This figure remained relatively stable
until 2000, when it began to decline sharply, falling to just 80
terawatt-hours by 2020. Natural gas started at 100 terawatt-hours and
rose steadily to reach a peak of 250 terawatt-hours in 2000, before
declining to 200 terawatt-hours by 2020.

Renewable energy sources, which contributed only 20 terawatt-hours in
1980, showed the most significant growth over the period. After a modest
increase to 50 terawatt-hours by 2000, the figure surged to 250
terawatt-hours by 2020, overtaking all other sources. Nuclear energy,
meanwhile, rose from 50 to 150 terawatt-hours between 1980 and 2000,
but subsequently fell back to 100 terawatt-hours by 2020.
```

**Word count**: 220

**Band score**: 7

**Examiner Commentary**:
```
Task Achievement (7): The response covers all key features and provides
a clear overview. Data is well-selected and supported with figures.
The overview identifies the main trends effectively.

Coherence and Cohesion (7): Information is logically organized with
clear paragraphing. Linking words are used appropriately ("In contrast",
"Meanwhile", "After"). The progression from highest to lowest is clear.

Lexical Resource (7): A good range of vocabulary is used ("dominant",
"dramatic growth", "trajectories", "surged"). Some less common items
are used with accuracy. No spelling errors.

Grammatical Range and Accuracy (7): A variety of complex structures
is used accurately. Passive voice and relative clauses are used
effectively. Minor errors do not impede communication.
```

**Key Strengths**:
- Clear overview identifying main trends
- Good use of data to support descriptions
- Varied vocabulary and sentence structures

**Areas for Improvement**:
- Could include more comparisons between sources
- The overview could be slightly more detailed

### Sample 2: Bar Chart (Band 8) | 范文2：柱状图（8分）

**Question**:
"The bar chart below shows the percentage of the population in urban areas in four different countries in 1950, 1990, and 2030 (projected)."

**Data summary**:
- Country A: 1950 (20%), 1990 (45%), 2030 (70%)
- Country B: 1950 (50%), 1990 (65%), 2030 (80%)
- Country C: 1950 (75%), 1990 (78%), 2030 (85%)
- Country D: 1950 (10%), 1990 (30%), 2030 (55%)

**Essay**:

```
The bar chart compares the proportion of people living in urban areas in
four countries across three time periods: 1950, 1990, and projected
figures for 2030.

Overall, urbanization increased in all four countries over the period,
though at varying rates. Country C consistently had the highest percentage
of urban residents, while Country D had the lowest. Notably, the gap
between the most and least urbanized countries widened significantly
over time.

In 1950, Country C was already highly urbanized at 75%, followed by
Country B at 50%. Country A and Country D had much lower figures, at
20% and 10% respectively. By 1990, all four countries had experienced
growth, with Country C rising slightly to 78%, Country B to 65%, Country
A to 45%, and Country D to 30%.

The projected figures for 2030 suggest that this trend will continue.
Country C is expected to reach 85%, maintaining its position as the most
urbanized nation. Country B is projected to reach 80%, while Country A
is forecast to more than triple its 1950 figure, reaching 70%. Perhaps
the most striking projection is for Country D, which is expected to
increase from just 10% in 1950 to 55% in 2030, representing a more
than fivefold increase over the 80-year period.
```

**Word count**: 208

**Band score**: 8

**Examiner Commentary**:
```
Task Achievement (8): All key features are fully covered. The overview
is excellent, identifying both the general trend and the notable widening
gap. Data is well-selected and accurately reported.

Coherence and Cohesion (8): The response is cohesively organized with
clear progression. Paragraphing is logical and each paragraph has a
clear focus. Cohesive devices are used naturally and appropriately.

Lexical Resource (8): A wide range of vocabulary is used with precision
("urbanized", "fivefold increase", "projected figures", "forecast").
Less common lexical items are used with full accuracy and naturalness.

Grammatical Range and Accuracy (8): A wide range of structures is used
with flexibility and accuracy. Complex sentences are error-free. The
writer demonstrates confident control of grammar.
```

**Key Strengths**:
- Excellent overview with insightful observation about widening gap
- Sophisticated vocabulary used naturally
- Wide range of accurate grammatical structures
- Good use of data without being excessive

## Task 2 Samples | 任务2范文

### Sample 3: Opinion Essay (Band 9) | 范文3：观点类文章（9分）

**Question**:
"Some people think that the best way to reduce crime is to give longer prison sentences. Others think there are better ways to reduce crime. Discuss both views and give your opinion."

**Essay**:

```
The question of how to most effectively reduce crime has long been a
subject of debate. While some advocate for longer prison sentences as
the primary solution, others argue that alternative approaches are more
effective. I believe that while incarceration has its place, investing
in prevention and rehabilitation yields far better results.

Those who support longer prison sentences argue that harsh punishment
serves as a powerful deterrent. The reasoning is straightforward: if
potential criminals know they will face severe consequences, they will
think twice before breaking the law. Furthermore, keeping offenders
behind bars for longer periods ensures public safety by preventing them
from committing further crimes during their sentence. For instance,
the "three strikes" law in California, which mandated life sentences
for repeat offenders, was initially credited with reducing crime rates
in the 1990s.

However, the evidence suggests that longer sentences alone are not the
most effective way to reduce crime. Firstly, many crimes are committed
impulsively or under the influence of substances, meaning that the
threat of punishment has little deterrent effect. Secondly, prison
environments often harden criminals rather than reform them, leading
to high recidivism rates upon release. In the United States, which has
one of the highest incarceration rates in the world, approximately 75%
of released prisoners are re-arrested within five years.

In my view, a more effective approach combines prevention with
rehabilitation. Investing in education, particularly in disadvantaged
communities, addresses the root causes of crime by providing young
people with legitimate opportunities for advancement. Additionally,
rehabilitation programmes that include vocational training, substance
abuse treatment, and psychological support have proven far more effective
at reducing reoffending. Norway's prison system, which emphasizes
rehabilitation over punishment, boasts a recidivism rate of just 20%,
compared to over 50% in most other developed nations.

In conclusion, while longer prison sentences may offer a sense of justice
and short-term safety, they fail to address the underlying causes of
criminal behaviour. A comprehensive strategy that prioritizes prevention
and rehabilitation is more likely to achieve lasting reductions in crime
rates and create safer communities for all.
```

**Word count**: 320

**Band score**: 9

**Examiner Commentary**:
```
Task Achievement (9): The response fully addresses all parts of the
question with a well-developed, balanced argument. The writer's position
is clear throughout and supported with relevant, extended examples. Ideas
are fully developed with logical reasoning.

Coherence and Cohesion (9): The essay is cohesively managed with seamless
transitions between ideas. Each paragraph has a clear central topic, and
ideas develop naturally. Cohesive devices are used with complete
naturalness and do not attract attention.

Lexical Resource (9): A wide range of vocabulary is used with full
flexibility and precision. Less common items are used naturally and
appropriately ("incarceration", "recidivism", "deterrent effect",
"rehabilitation"). There are no errors.

Grammatical Range and Accuracy (9): A wide range of structures is used
with complete accuracy and flexibility. Complex sentences are used
naturally and effectively. There are virtually no errors.
```

**Key Strengths**:
- Fully developed arguments with specific examples (California, USA, Norway)
- Clear position maintained throughout
- Sophisticated vocabulary and grammar
- Excellent logical flow and cohesion

### Sample 4: Discussion Essay (Band 7) | 范文4：讨论类文章（7分）

**Question**:
"Some people think that the internet has brought people closer together, while others think it has pushed them further apart. Discuss both views and give your own opinion."

**Essay**:

```
The internet has transformed the way people communicate and interact
with each other. While some people believe that this technology has
brought people closer together, others argue that it has created more
distance between individuals. This essay will discuss both perspectives
before presenting my own opinion.

On the one hand, those who believe the internet brings people together
have valid reasons. The most obvious benefit is that it allows people
to stay in touch with friends and family regardless of geographical
distance. For example, video calling applications like Zoom and Skype
enable people to have face-to-face conversations with loved ones on
the other side of the world. Furthermore, social media platforms allow
people to maintain large networks of friends and acquaintances that
would be impossible to keep in touch with through traditional means.

On the other hand, critics argue that the internet has actually made
people more isolated. They contend that online interactions are often
superficial and cannot replace genuine human connection. For instance,
many young people spend hours on social media but report feeling lonely
and disconnected from those around them. Moreover, the rise of remote
working and online shopping has reduced the need for face-to-face
interactions in daily life, leading to less social contact overall.

In my opinion, the internet has both connected and disconnected people
in different ways. While it has made long-distance communication easier
and more accessible, it has also reduced the quality of local
relationships. I believe that the key is to use the internet as a
supplement to, rather than a replacement for, real-world interactions.
People who maintain a healthy balance between online and offline
relationships are most likely to benefit from the connectivity that
the internet offers.

In conclusion, the internet has both positive and negative effects on
human connection. While it enables us to communicate across distances,
it can also reduce the depth of our local relationships. The impact of
the internet on human connection ultimately depends on how individuals
choose to use it.
```

**Word count**: 293

**Band score**: 7

**Examiner Commentary**:
```
Task Achievement (7): Both views are clearly discussed with relevant
supporting points. The writer's opinion is clear and well-supported.
However, the examples could be more specific and detailed.

Coherence and Cohesion (7): The essay is well-organized with clear
paragraphing. Linking words are used appropriately. Each paragraph
has a clear focus. The conclusion effectively summarizes the main points.

Lexical Resource (7): A good range of vocabulary is used ("superficial",
"geographical distance", "connectivity"). There is some repetition of
words like "internet" and "people". Less common items are used with
generally good accuracy.

Grammatical Range and Accuracy (7): A variety of complex structures
is used. There are some minor errors, but these do not impede
communication. The writer demonstrates good control of grammar overall.
```

**Key Strengths**:
- Clear structure with both views discussed
- Good use of examples (Zoom, social media)
- Clear personal opinion stated

**Areas for Improvement**:
- Examples could be more specific and detailed
- Some vocabulary repetition
- Could use more sophisticated grammatical structures

### Sample 5: Problem-Solution Essay (Band 6) | 范文5：问题解决类文章（6分）

**Question**:
"In many cities, the number of people using public transport is declining. What are the causes of this problem, and what solutions can you suggest?"

**Essay**:

```
Public transport usage has been decreasing in many cities around the
world. This essay will discuss the reasons for this problem and suggest
some solutions.

There are several reasons why fewer people are using public transport.
Firstly, many public transport systems are old and unreliable. Buses
and trains are often late, which makes people frustrated. For example,
in my city, the buses are always late and this makes people choose to
drive instead. Secondly, public transport is sometimes more expensive
than driving, especially for families. When you have to buy tickets for
a whole family, it can cost a lot of money.

Another reason is that people prefer the comfort and convenience of
their own cars. Cars are more comfortable than crowded buses and trains.
Also, you can go anywhere you want at any time without waiting. This
is especially important for people who work late at night when public
transport is not available.

To solve these problems, governments should invest more money in public
transport. They should buy new buses and trains that are comfortable
and reliable. Also, they should make public transport cheaper by
reducing ticket prices or offering monthly passes at discounted rates.
For example, in many European cities, monthly transport passes are very
affordable and this encourages more people to use public transport.

Another solution is to make public transport more convenient. Governments
should increase the frequency of buses and trains, especially during
peak hours. They should also extend the operating hours so that people
who work late can still use public transport.

In conclusion, the decline in public transport usage is caused by
unreliable services, high costs, and the comfort of private cars. However,
by investing in better infrastructure and making public transport more
affordable and convenient, governments can encourage more people to use
it.
```

**Word count**: 273

**Band score**: 6

**Examiner Commentary**:
```
Task Achievement (6): The response addresses both causes and solutions.
The main points are relevant, but some ideas are underdeveloped. The
solutions are somewhat generic and could be more specific.

Coherence and Cohesion (6): There is a clear overall structure. However,
the paragraphing could be improved — some paragraphs contain multiple
ideas. Linking words are used but sometimes repetitively ("Also", "For
example").

Lexical Resource (5-6): The vocabulary is adequate but limited. There
is significant repetition ("public transport" appears many times). The
language is sometimes too informal ("a lot of money", "my city").

Grammatical Range and Accuracy (6): Simple and compound sentences are
used accurately. Complex structures are attempted but with some errors.
The range is limited.
```

**Key Weaknesses**:
- Examples are too general and personal ("in my city")
- Vocabulary is repetitive
- Language is sometimes too informal
- Ideas could be more fully developed

**How to Improve to Band 7**:
1. Use more specific, real-world examples
2. Vary vocabulary (use synonyms for "public transport")
3. Use more formal language
4. Develop ideas more fully with detailed explanations
